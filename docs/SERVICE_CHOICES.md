# Service choices

Why each Azure service in this product was chosen over its nearest alternative. One to three rows are added per build session.

| Session | Scenario | Chosen | Rejected alternative | When the alternative wins | Exam trap | Exam skill | What a client would ask |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S00 | Residents chat with the service desk during the day. Traffic is low, spiky and live, so answers must come in seconds with no commitment. | Global Standard (pay per token) | Global Batch, Global Provisioned, Data Zone Standard | Batch wins for bulk work that can wait up to 24 hours at about half the price, such as scoring a whole month of tickets. Provisioned wins when throughput and latency must be reserved, for example a call centre with steady heavy load, accepting that reserved capacity is billed whether used or not. Data Zone wins when a client's rules say processing must stay inside a defined data zone (check which zones suit Dubai clients in S01, ADR-001). | Batch is cheaper but is wrong for live chat. Provisioned guarantees capacity, not a quoted uptime figure. | 1.2.b, 1.2.c | If our volume doubles, what do we pay, and where is our data processed? |
| S00 | The app signs in to the Foundry project from a laptop today and from a deployed service later. | Keyless sign-in with Microsoft Entra ID (`DefaultAzureCredential`) and the Foundry User role | API key | A key wins only when the caller cannot use Entra, such as an older tool or a third-party service that accepts only a key, or for a short throwaway test. Frequent or independent calls are not a reason, because the SDK refreshes tokens by itself. | Key-based calls bypass role-based access control. Owner and Contributor do not include Foundry data permissions, so the Foundry User role is still needed. | 1.3.d | Who called the model, and can you cut off one person's access without changing anything for others? |
